Cross-site tracking rarely passes legitimate interest

GDPR · Personalisation · EU Guidance
What It Means

The European Data Protection Board adopted final guidelines on legitimate interest on 10 November. Marketing that tracks people across websites, devices or services is very unlikely to pass the balancing test. Common practice in a sector does not by itself make processing expected. Email and SMS marketing still needs prior consent, with an exception for your own customers. EDPB guidelines (PDF)

My VerdictAct Now

If your personalisation runs on legitimate interest, list every data source that feeds each profile. Purchases in your own shop and your own channels have a case. Signals from other websites, devices or partner networks will almost certainly need consent. Fix the legal basis before your DPO asks.

ChatGPT agents now pay, with approval on each purchase

AI Commerce · Payments · OpenAI
What It Means

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on 13 November for ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US. Agents pay with a network token instead of the real card details. Users approve each purchase with a security check such as Face ID. Businesses accept the token through existing checkout systems and keep the customer relationship. Card rewards and protections for eligible disputes stay. OpenAI

My VerdictWatch

Agent orders will reach your checkout looking like ordinary card payments. Ask your payment provider how agent tokens show up in your order data. Then decide how those orders count for sales attribution, paid media budgets and loyalty points. Otherwise the channels that earned the sale get no credit.
